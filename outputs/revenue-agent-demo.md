## Why the Topic Should Work

**Evidence** (national small-business data; not Biloxi-specific):
- 43% of small businesses struggle to create content consistently.
- 65% struggle with the budget or time for marketing.
- 54% can't tell whether their marketing works.
- About 90% of restaurant operators call social media very or extremely important, yet doing it right takes 5-7+ hours a week.

**Inference** (not confirmed locally): Biloxi owner-operators, with small staffs and no in-house marketer, likely have the same gap or a worse one.

**Audience psychology:**
- **Want:** customers walking in or booking.
- **Fear:** paying someone who posts for two weeks and disappears.
- **Misunderstand:** they think the problem is the platform, the tool, or the trend. It's actually the lack of a repeatable system.
- **Secretly hope:** for a low-risk test with a local person who actually follows through.

**Distribution note:** owners don't search YouTube for "hire a social media manager" (searchability scored 3/5). Treat this video as interrupt content: share it in local Facebook groups, send it in DMs, and show it in person, not only on YouTube.

## The Unique Mechanism

**The 3-step system: Quality Content + Consistent Posting + Responding to People = Real Customers.**

1. **Quality content:** built for one defined ideal customer. A gym targets "busy parents who want a 30-minute workout," not "everyone who likes fitness."
2. **Consistent posting:** a visible weekly calendar with no gaps.
3. **Responding to people:** every comment and DM answered within 24 hours. This is where attention turns into a visit.

**The line that carries the video:** "The money isn't in the tools. It's in the system. Anyone can post. Almost nobody follows through."

**Why it's believable coming from Lenny:** military discipline and a sales background explain *why* the follow-through happens. Those are "why me" reasons, not results claims.

## 5-DM Follow-Up Sequence

> The coordinator prompt asked for emails. The runbook's business rules require DMs, because leads come from comments and DMs. Lenny sends every message by hand on the platform where the person reached out. Never automate these; platforms can ban accounts for automated DMs.

**DM 1: Confirm and collect** (within 24 hours of their comment)
> "Hey [Name]! Saw you commented 'IN' on the video. Appreciate it. I'd love to run that free 10-minute audit on your page. Can you drop me the link to your [Facebook/Instagram/TikTok] page so I can take a look?"

**DM 2: Deliver the audit** (no ask attached)
> "Alright, took a real look at [Business Name]'s page. Recorded you a quick 10-minute walkthrough, same 3 steps from the video, applied to your actual page. [attach video/voice memo] Let me know what stands out to you."

**DM 3: Invite a short call** (only after they react to DM 2)
> "Glad that was useful. If you want, I can hop on a quick 10-15 minute call and walk through what a full month of this would look like for [Business Name]. No obligation either way. What works for you this week?"

**DM 4: Confirm the free month** (after the call)
> "Good talking today. Like I mentioned, I'm only doing this for the first 3 businesses, and you'd be taking one of those spots: first month completely free, no contract. If you're good with it, I'll start building your first week of content. Want me to go ahead?"

**DM 5: Gentle follow-up** (about 5-7 days after no response)
> "Hey [Name], no worries if now's not the right time. The free-month spots are limited to 3 businesses, so if you want one, just let me know. If you'd rather pick this up later, I'm here. Either way, that audit's yours to keep."

*Coordinator note:* the original DM 5 said spots were held "a bit longer," which is a deadline that doesn't exist. It now states only the true limit.

**Sales path:** comment "IN" or DM -> DM 1 -> audit (DM 2) -> call (DM 3) -> free month (DM 4) -> day-30 decision -> $500/month.

**Friction check:**
- Deliver the audit **before** asking for a call, or it feels like bait-and-switch.
- In DM 1, ask only for the page link. No phone number, email, or form.
- Reply on the platform where they reached out. Don't chase them across apps.
- Track the 3 spots in a simple note. If a spot is gone, say so plainly.
- Give one CTA in the video. Don't also ask viewers to subscribe, follow, and share.
- Keep DM 5 low-key. No "spots filling fast!!"

## What the Creator Must Show on Screen to Be Credible

- **The whole 3-step system running live:** building a real post, the real weekly calendar, a real reply being sent.
- **The caption line that names the ideal customer.** Zoom in on it; this is the "targeting, not everyone" proof.
- **Sample content on the agency's own page,** so viewers see the quality bar.
- **A real local "before" page, shown respectfully,** with the name and logo blurred unless the owner agreed to be shown.
- **The honesty line, said early:** "I don't have clients yet."
- **Veteran and sales background, stated plainly** as the reason for the discipline, not as proof of results.
- **A promise to document the first 3 clients' real results publicly,** once they exist.

**Coordinator corrections for credibility:**
- If the agency page has no real DMs yet, reply to a real comment, or have a friend send a DM and **say on camera** that it's a demo. Never stage a fake inbox.
- Blur or get permission for any real business shown. Embarrassing a local business in public could cost you that business as a client and hurt your reputation in a small market.

## What to Remove Because It Feels Generic

- Any line like "social media will grow your business" or "you need to be on social media." They already know that.
- Tips lists ("5 tips for better posts"). Show the system instead.
- Tool talk (apps, schedulers, AI tools, hashtag tricks). The money is in the system, not the tools.
- "Engagement," "reach," and "followers" as goals. Say "customers" instead.
- Any implied result: before/after numbers, "clients are seeing," stock testimonials, or client logos.
- Fake urgency beyond the true 3-spot limit.
- Stacked CTAs (subscribe + follow + share + DM).
- Promising stories on YouTube Shorts or X, which have no stories feature.
